# Santorini

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A volcanic island with ancient vines trained into low baskets, producing mineral, high-acid Assyrtiko.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Greece]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** volcanic, pumice, ash, basalt
> - **Main grapes:** [[Assyrtiko]], [[Athiri]], [[Aidani]], [[Mandilaria]]

## Landscape and topography
A crescent-shaped caldera island, with low, flat vineyards and spectacular cliffs.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic ash, pumice and lava from the huge Minoan eruption around 1600 BC. Phylloxera never took hold in the sandy volcanic soil, so many vines are ungrafted and very old.

## Climate
Hot, dry and extremely windy, with little rain. Vines are trained in koulouras (basket shapes) to protect from wind and sun, and the grapes get moisture from morning mist.

## History
Vines have been grown on Santorini for over 3,000 years. Vinsanto, a sweet wine from sun-dried grapes, has a long history. The wine industry has boomed with tourism.

## Production and wine styles
Assyrtiko leads for dry whites, with Athiri and Aidani in blends. Vinsanto is made from sun-dried Assyrtiko and Aidani. Nykteri is a traditional barrel-aged style.

## Key wines
- Santorini Assyrtiko
- Nykteri
- Vinsanto

## What to look for
- Lemon, salt, volcanic smoke and very high acidity.
- A superb match for grilled fish and seafood.
