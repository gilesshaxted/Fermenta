# Côte Chalonnaise

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A more affordable stretch of Burgundy south of the Côte d'Or, with pretty reds and good-value whites.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Burgundy]]
> - **Climate:** Semi-continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, clay, marl
> - **Main grapes:** [[Pinot Noir]], [[Chardonnay]], [[Aligoté]]

## Landscape and topography
Lower, more fragmented hills than the Côte d'Or, with vineyards scattered among farmland.

## Soil and geology
Limestone and clay, with some marl, giving a range of styles from village to village.

## Climate
Semi-continental, a little warmer and less frost-prone than the Côte d'Or.

## History
Bouzeron was awarded its own village appellation in 1998, the only village dedicated to Aligoté.

## Production and wine styles
Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, with Aligoté at Bouzeron. Sparkling Crémant de Bourgogne is also made here.

## Key wines
- Mercurey
- Rully
- Givry
- Montagny
- Bouzeron

## What to look for
- Often good value and attractive earlier than Côte d'Or wines.
- Mercurey and Givry offer red, Montagny is white only.
