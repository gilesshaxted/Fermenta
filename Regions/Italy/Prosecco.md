# Prosecco

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Italy's best-known sparkling wine, with the finest bottles from the steep hills of Conegliano Valdobbiadene.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Veneto]]
> - **Climate:** Temperate
> - **Soils:** clay, marl, sandstone, gravel
> - **Main grapes:** [[Glera]]
> - **Styles:** [[Prosecco (style)|Prosecco style]], [[Charmat method]]

## Landscape and topography
Gently steep hills between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, with vine terraces called ciglioni, and wider plains in the DOC area.

## Soil and geology
Marl, clay and sandstone on the hills, flatter alluvial soils on the plains.

## Climate
Temperate, with Alpine air giving cool nights.

## History
Prosecco is a grape-turned-place. Conegliano's wine school, the oldest in Italy, was founded in 1876. The hills became a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2019.

## Production and wine styles
Glera, made in tanks by the Charmat method (second fermentation in a tank). Conegliano Valdobbiadene is DOCG and Asolo is a smaller DOCG. Rive and Cartizze are the top vineyard designations.

## Key wines
- Prosecco DOC
- Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco Superiore DOCG
- Cartizze

## What to look for
- Pear, apple and white flowers, light and fresh.
- Extra Dry is actually a touch sweeter than Brut.
