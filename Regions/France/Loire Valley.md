# Loire Valley

> [!abstract] At a glance
> France's longest river, running from the Atlantic to the centre of the country, with fresh, high-acid wines in a huge range of styles.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** tuffeau, schist, limestone, flint, gravel
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chenin Blanc]], [[Sauvignon Blanc]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Melon de Bourgogne]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Gamay]]
> - **Styles:** [[Crémant]], [[Pétillant naturel]], [[Traditional method]], [[Natural wine]]

## Landscape and topography
A long river corridor from the Atlantic coast at Nantes to the hills of Sancerre in central France, with gentle valleys, tuffeau cliffs and rolling plateaux.

## Soil and geology
Varies by section: granite and schist in the west, tuffeau limestone and clay-flint in the middle, and limestone, marl and flint in the east.

## Climate
Cool, with Atlantic influence in the west and a more continental feel in the east. Fresh acidity is the thread running through the wines.

## History
Known as the Garden of France and for its châteaux, the Loire was a royal retreat. Wines were shipped down the river to Paris and northern Europe, and notably to the Netherlands.

## Production and wine styles
Muscadet (Melon de Bourgogne) in the west, Chenin Blanc in Anjou, Saumur and Touraine, Cabernet Franc in Chinon, Bourgueil and Saumur-Champigny, and Sauvignon Blanc around Sancerre and Pouilly. Styles run from dry to sweet to sparkling.

## Key wines
- Muscadet
- Vouvray
- Sancerre
- Chinon
- Savennières
- Coteaux du Layon
- Crémant de Loire

## What to look for
- Think of it in four sections: Nantes, Anjou-Saumur, Touraine and the Central Vineyards.
- Cooler, higher-acid wines make the Loire a natural partner for seafood and goat's cheese.
