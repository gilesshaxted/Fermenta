# Bolgheri

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A coastal strip that created the Super Tuscan with Sassicaia and the Bordeaux-style blend.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Tuscany]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** gravel, clay, sand, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Merlot]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Syrah]], [[Petit Verdot]]
> - **Styles:** [[Super Tuscan]]

## Landscape and topography
A flat to gently sloping coastal plain between the Tyrrhenian Sea and the hills, lined with cypress trees.

## Soil and geology
Gravel, sand and clay on the plain, with stonier, more complex soils further up the slope.

## Climate
Warm Mediterranean, with sea breezes and cooling hill air at night.

## History
In the 1940s Marchese Mario Incisa della Rocca planted Cabernet at Tenuta San Guido and made Sassicaia, which was first sold commercially in 1968 and famously beat Bordeaux in tastings. Bolgheri DOC followed in 1983.

## Production and wine styles
Bordeaux grapes lead the blends, with Bolgheri Superiore the top tier. Bolgheri white wines use Vermentino and Sauvignon Blanc.

## Key wines
- Bolgheri Rosso
- Bolgheri Superiore
- Sassicaia (Bolgheri Sassicaia DOC)
- Ornellaia
- Masseto (Merlot)

## What to look for
- Cassis, cedar, herbs and Mediterranean warmth.
- Bolgheri Rosso is a more affordable entry point.
