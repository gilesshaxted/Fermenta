# Tuscany

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Italy's most famous wine region, with Sangiovese in Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and the Super Tuscans of the coast.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** clay, limestone, galestro, alberese, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sangiovese]], [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Merlot]], [[Vernaccia]], [[Trebbiano]]
> - **Styles:** [[Vin Santo]], [[Super Tuscan]]

## Landscape and topography
Rolling hills, cypress-lined ridges and a western coastline, with vineyards at roughly 100 to 600 metres.

## Soil and geology
Galestro (crumbly schistous clay) and alberese (hard limestone) in the Chianti hills, volcanic soils at Montalcino's edge, and gravel and sand on the coast.

## Climate
Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and cooler, wetter winters. Altitude and sea breezes moderate temperatures.

## History
Etruscans made wine here. Chianti's recipe was set in the 19th century by Baron Ricasoli. In the 1970s producers such as Sassicaia and Tignanello broke the rules with Cabernet and barrique, creating the Super Tuscans, and prompting the creation of the IGT category.

## Production and wine styles
Sangiovese is the backbone, with Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah in coastal blends. White wines include Vernaccia di San Gimignano and Vermentino. Sweet Vin Santo is a local speciality.

## Key wines
- Chianti Classico
- Brunello di Montalcino
- Vino Nobile di Montepulciano
- Bolgheri Superiore
- Super Tuscans
- Vernaccia di San Gimignano

## What to look for
- Learn the Sangiovese names: Chianti, Brunello (Montalcino), Vino Nobile (Montepulciano).
- Super Tuscan is not a legal term, so check the producer.
