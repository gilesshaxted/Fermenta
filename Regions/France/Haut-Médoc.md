# Haut-Médoc

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The southern Médoc, containing the great communes and the broader appellation that surrounds them.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Bordeaux]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** gravel, clay, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Merlot]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Petit Verdot]]
> - **Styles:** [[Bordeaux blend]]

## Landscape and topography
Gentle gravel mounds and low plateaux close to the Gironde. Margaux, Saint-Julien, Pauillac and Saint-Estèphe are separate communal appellations inside it, alongside Listrac and Moulis.

## Soil and geology
Deep gravel over clay and limestone, giving excellent drainage and heat retention.

## Climate
Maritime, with warmth held by the gravel and the estuary moderating cold and frost.

## History
The Haut-Médoc is the home of the 1855 Classification, and most of its red châteaux sit in the communes listed here. The plain Haut-Médoc appellation also includes several classified growths.

## Production and wine styles
Cabernet Sauvignon leads, blended with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. Red only.

## Key wines
- Haut-Médoc
- Listrac-Médoc
- Moulis-en-Médoc

## What to look for
- The communal name on the label (Margaux, Pauillac and so on) tells you the style.
- Plain Haut-Médoc can offer reliable value, with some classified estates among them.
