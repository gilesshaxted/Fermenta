# Beaujolais

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Light, juicy Gamay reds from granite hills, from simple fruity Beaujolais to serious age-worthy Crus.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Semi-continental
> - **Soils:** granite, schist, clay, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Gamay]], [[Chardonnay]]
> - **Styles:** [[Pétillant naturel]], [[Natural wine]], [[Carbonic maceration]]

## Landscape and topography
A band of rolling hills at the southern end of Burgundy, with the best vineyards on steep granite slopes in the north.

## Soil and geology
Granite and schist in the north, where the Crus sit, and clay and limestone in the south.

## Climate
Semi-continental, with a warmer, sunnier feel than Burgundy proper.

## History
Beaujolais Nouveau, released on the third Thursday of November, became a marketing phenomenon in the 1970s and 1980s. A quality revival has since focused on the ten Crus.

## Production and wine styles
Gamay, often made by carbonic or semi-carbonic maceration for fruity, low-tannin wines. A little white Beaujolais is made from Chardonnay.

## Key wines
- Beaujolais
- Beaujolais-Villages
- The ten Crus
- Beaujolais Nouveau

## What to look for
- Red fruit and a bubblegum or banana note from carbonic maceration in simple wines.
- Lightly chilled, Beaujolais is a wonderfully versatile food wine.
