# Valle de Guadalupe

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Mexico's leading quality region, in northern Baja California, with a Mediterranean climate close to the Pacific.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Mexico]]
> - **Part of:** [[North America]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** granite, sandy loam, alluvial, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Nebbiolo]], [[Tempranillo]], [[Grenache]], [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Chenin Blanc]], [[Petite Sirah]]

## Landscape and topography
A valley inland from Ensenada, with vineyards on valley floors and surrounding hills, cooled by Pacific air.

## Soil and geology
Granite based sandy soils, alluvial deposits and some clay.

## Climate
Dry and warm with cool ocean influence, similar to parts of California. Water is a key limit on growth.

## History
Wine making in Baja California began in the twentieth century and expanded as new private wineries opened. The valley is now a popular food and wine destination.

## Production and wine styles
Bold reds from Cabernet, Tempranillo, Grenache, Nebbiolo and blends, plus fresh whites such as Chenin Blanc.

## Key wines
- Valle de Guadalupe red blends
- Baja Nebbiolo
- Baja Tempranillo

## What to look for
- Most of Mexico's quality wine comes from Baja California.
- Wines often blend several varieties.
