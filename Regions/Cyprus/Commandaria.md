# Commandaria

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Cyprus's famous sweet wine, made from sun-dried grapes on the southern slopes of the Troodos mountains, and one of the oldest named wines still made.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Cyprus]]
> - **Part of:** [[Troodos]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** chalk, limestone, marl
> - **Main grapes:** [[Xynisteri]], [[Mavro]]

## Landscape and topography
A group of villages on the southern slopes of the Troodos mountains, in the hills above Limassol.

## Soil and geology
Chalky, limestone-rich soils, often poor and stony.

## Climate
Hot, dry summers with sunshine to dry the grapes, with some relief from altitude.

## History
Commandaria takes its name from the commanderies of the Knights Hospitaller and Templar in the Middle Ages, and the wine has been famous for centuries. It is a protected name and is only made in a defined zone.

## Production and wine styles
A sweet, amber to dark brown wine from Xynisteri and Mavro, with the grapes dried in the sun before pressing and the wine aged in barrel.

## Key wines
- Commandaria

## What to look for
- Rich notes of raisin, caramel and spice.
- Try it with nuts or blue cheese.
