# Valais

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Switzerland's largest wine canton, a sunny, dry Alpine valley that is home to many of the country's rare native grapes.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Switzerland]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** schist, granite, limestone, gravel, loess
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chasselas]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Gamay]], [[Petite Arvine]], [[Cornalin]], [[Syrah]]

## Landscape and topography
A long stretch of the upper Rhône valley, with steep terraced vineyards from about 450 to over 1,000 metres, sheltered by high mountains.

## Soil and geology
Very varied, from schist and granite to limestone, gravel and wind-blown loess, often within a few kilometres.

## Climate
One of the driest and sunniest parts of Switzerland, so many vineyards need irrigation, traditionally from mountain channels called bisses.

## History
Vines have been grown here since Roman times at least. The canton later became the home of unusual local grapes such as Petite Arvine, Humagne Rouge and Cornalin.

## Production and wine styles
Dry whites led by Chasselas, known locally as Fendant, plus Petite Arvine and Humagne Blanche. Reds include Dôle, a blend of Pinot Noir and Gamay, and single-variety Cornalin, Humagne Rouge and Syrah, with some sweet wines too.

## Key wines
- Fendant
- Dôle
- Petite Arvine
- Cornalin
- Humagne Rouge

## What to look for
- Fendant is the classic Alpine white.
- Seek out the local grapes, which rarely leave Switzerland.
