# Grenache

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Ripe red fruit and warm spice with soft tannins and plenty of alcohol. A Mediterranean staple, often blended and often made into rosé.

## Where it's from
Probably Spain, where it is called Garnacha. It spread across the western Mediterranean and is grown in Sardinia as Cannonau.

## Where it grows
- **Southern Rhône, France:** the main grape of Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Gigondas
- **Priorat and Rioja, Spain:** old bush vines, powerful and concentrated
- **Sardinia, Italy:** known as Cannonau
- **Languedoc-Roussillon, France:** sunny, spicy reds and rosé
- **McLaren Vale, Australia:** old-vine, ripe and generous

## What it tastes like
- **Fresh styles:** raspberry, strawberry, white pepper
- **Ripe styles:** kirsch, jammy red fruit, liquorice
- **Savoury notes:** dried herbs, orange peel, warm spice

## Classic wines
- Châteauneuf-du-Pape
- Gigondas
- Priorat
- Cannonau di Sardegna
- Provence and Tavel rosé
- GSM blends

## Pairs with
- Roast lamb
- Tagine
- Sausages
- Tapas and charcuterie
- Tomato-based dishes

## Good to know
- Often looks pale in the glass yet is high in alcohol.
- A big part of southern French rosé. Garnacha in Spain, Cannonau in Sardinia, Grenache in France and Australia.
