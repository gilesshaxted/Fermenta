# Solera system

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A way of ageing by fractional blending, where younger wine tops up older wine.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Category:** Method
> - **Colour:** Varies
> - **Sweetness:** Varies
> - **Country:** Spain
> - **Main grapes:** [[Palomino]], [[Pedro Ximénez]]
> - **Regions:** [[Jerez]], [[Marsala]]

## How it's made
Barrels are arranged in tiers. Wine is drawn from the oldest tier, the solera, and topped up from the tier above, which is topped up from the one above that. This gives consistency, and each bottle holds some very old wine.

## Where it's made
Used mainly in Sherry and Marsala, and in some other fortified and aged wines.

## What it tastes like
- Consistent house style
- Nutty, aged complexity

## Pairs with
- Depends on the wine

## Good to know
- Only a fraction (often a third) of any barrel is removed each time.
- A solera can hold wine from many decades.
