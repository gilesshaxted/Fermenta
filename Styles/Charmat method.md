# Charmat method

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Sparkling wine made by a second fermentation in a large sealed tank.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Category:** Method
> - **Colour:** Varies
> - **Sweetness:** Varies
> - **Country:** Italy
> - **Main grapes:** [[Glera]], [[Muscat]], [[Lambrusco]]
> - **Regions:** [[Prosecco]], [[Asti]], [[Modena and Reggio Emilia]]

## How it's made
Base wine goes into a pressurised tank with sugar and yeast. After the bubbles form the wine is filtered and bottled under pressure. This is quicker and cheaper and keeps the primary fruit.

## Where it's made
Used for Prosecco, Asti, Lambrusco and many fresh, aromatic sparkling wines.

## What it tastes like
- Fresh fruit and floral aromas
- Light, frothy bubbles

## Pairs with
- Aperitif
- Light starters

## Good to know
- Named after Eugène Charmat, who patented the process in 1907.
- Best for aromatic grapes where lees flavours are unwanted.
