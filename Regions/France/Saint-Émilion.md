# Saint-Émilion

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A medieval hilltop town surrounded by limestone slopes, known for soft, plummy Merlot-led reds.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Bordeaux]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** limestone, clay, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Merlot]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Cabernet Sauvignon]]
> - **Styles:** [[Bordeaux blend]]

## Landscape and topography
A limestone plateau, steep slopes (côtes) and sandy lower ground on the right bank of the Dordogne.

## Soil and geology
Limestone and clay on the plateau and slopes, sand and gravel on lower ground, with iron-rich clay in places.

## Climate
Maritime, slightly warmer and drier than the Left Bank.

## History
The town and its jurisdiction became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999. Its classification is revised roughly every ten years, and has been controversial.

## Production and wine styles
Merlot and Cabernet Franc lead, with some Cabernet Sauvignon. Estates are small compared with the Médoc, and 'garage' wines emerged in the 1990s.

## Key wines
- Château Cheval Blanc
- Château Ausone
- Château Figeac
- Château Angélus

## What to look for
- The label ranges from plain Saint-Émilion to Grand Cru to Grand Cru Classé, so read it carefully.
- Plum, cherry, cocoa and a rounder style than the Left Bank.
