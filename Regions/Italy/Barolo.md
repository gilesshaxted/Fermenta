# Barolo

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The king of Italian wines: tannic, perfumed Nebbiolo from eleven villages around the town of Barolo.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Piedmont]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** marl, limestone, sandstone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Nebbiolo]]

## Landscape and topography
A tangle of steep hills in the Langhe, with named vineyards (MGAs) on south, south-east and south-west facing slopes.

## Soil and geology
Two main soils. Tortonian marl to the west (La Morra, Barolo) gives softer, fragrant wines. Serravallian sandstone to the east (Serralunga, Monforte) gives firmer, longer-lived wines.

## Climate
Continental, with fog in autumn and strong sun on the slopes. Nebbiolo is picked late, often in October.

## History
The wine owes its modern form to the 19th century, when it became dry. Aged-in-large-barrel (traditional) and barrique (modern) camps divided producers in the 1980s.

## Production and wine styles
100% Nebbiolo. Minimum ageing is 38 months, with at least 18 in wood (Riserva is 62 months). The 170 or so named MGAs now appear on labels.

## Key wines
- Barolo
- Barolo Riserva
- Barolo single-vineyard (MGA) wines

## What to look for
- Rose, tar, cherry, truffle and dried herbs.
- Villages differ: La Morra is elegant, Serralunga is powerful.
