# Jerez

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The home of Sherry, a fortified wine made in a triangle of towns in southern Spain.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Spain]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** albariza, chalk, sand, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Palomino]], [[Pedro Ximénez]], [[Muscat|Moscatel]]
> - **Styles:** [[Sherry]], [[Solera system]]

## Landscape and topography
Rolling land near the Atlantic, with the Sherry Triangle of Jerez, Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María.

## Soil and geology
Albariza, a bright white chalky soil that holds winter rain and reflects sun.

## Climate
Warm Mediterranean with Atlantic breezes (the poniente and levante winds).

## History
Phoenicians and Romans made wine here. English merchants fuelled the sherry trade from the 16th century. Francis Drake's raid on Cádiz in 1587 took 2,900 butts back to England.

## Production and wine styles
Fortified wine aged in a solera system of fractional blending. Palomino makes dry styles, and Pedro Ximénez or Moscatel make sweet ones. Fino and Manzanilla age under flor yeast, while Oloroso ages oxidatively.

## Key wines
- Fino
- Manzanilla
- Amontillado
- Oloroso
- Palo Cortado
- Pedro Ximénez
- Cream Sherry

## What to look for
- Fino and Manzanilla are bone dry and served chilled.
- Pedro Ximénez is a dark, sticky dessert wine.
