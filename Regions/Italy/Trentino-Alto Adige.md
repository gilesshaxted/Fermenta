# Trentino-Alto Adige

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A bilingual Alpine region where high-altitude vineyards make crisp whites and local reds.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Alpine
> - **Soils:** porphyry, limestone, dolomite, moraine, alluvium
> - **Main grapes:** [[Pinot Gris|Pinot Grigio]], [[Lagrein]], [[Teroldego]], [[Pinot Blanc]], [[Gewürztraminer]], [[Chardonnay]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep slopes, valleys and terraces in the Dolomites and Alps, with vineyards at up to 1,000 metres.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic porphyry, limestone, dolomite and glacial moraine, depending on the valley.

## Climate
Alpine, with cool nights, warm days and plenty of sunshine, which keeps white wines fresh.

## History
Once part of Austria until 1919, South Tyrol (Alto Adige) is still bilingual German and Italian. Gewürztraminer takes its name from the village of Tramin (Termeno).

## Production and wine styles
Alto Adige is mostly white (Pinot Grigio, Pinot Bianco, Gewürztraminer, Sauvignon), with Lagrein and Pinot Nero for red. Trentino adds Teroldego, Nosiola, and Trento DOC sparkling wine.

## Key wines
- Alto Adige (Südtirol)
- Trentino
- Trento DOC
- Lagrein
- Teroldego Rotaliano

## What to look for
- Crisp, mineral and aromatic.
- Good value, especially cooperatives.
