# Veneto

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Italy's biggest quality-wine producer by volume, home of Prosecco, Amarone and Soave.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Temperate
> - **Soils:** limestone, volcanic, clay, alluvium, gravel
> - **Main grapes:** [[Corvina]], [[Garganega]], [[Glera]], [[Pinot Gris]], [[Merlot]]
> - **Styles:** [[Prosecco (style)|Prosecco style]], [[Passito]], [[Amarone]]

## Landscape and topography
Plains, foothills of the Alps and rolling hills around Lake Garda, Verona and Treviso.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic basalt in Soave, limestone in Valpolicella, alluvial gravel and clay on the plains.

## Climate
Temperate, with Alpine influence in the north and the moderating effect of Lake Garda.

## History
Venice's trading power shaped the region's wine history. Prosecco became a global success from the 2000s, with its main hills added to UNESCO status in 2019.

## Production and wine styles
Prosecco (Glera) sparkling, Soave (Garganega), Valpolicella and Amarone (Corvina, Rondinella, Molinara), Bardolino, and Pinot Grigio from the plains.

## Key wines
- Prosecco
- Amarone della Valpolicella
- Valpolicella Ripasso
- Soave Classico
- Bardolino

## What to look for
- Prosecco quality ranges from simple to hillside Conegliano Valdobbiadene.
- Amarone is made from dried grapes for a rich, powerful style.
