# German-speaking Switzerland

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A scattering of cantons in northern and eastern Switzerland, best known for Pinot Noir and for Müller-Thurgau.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Switzerland]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, molasse, clay, gravel, moraine
> - **Main grapes:** [[Pinot Noir]], [[Müller-Thurgau]], [[Pinot Gris]], [[Chardonnay]], [[Sylvaner]]

## Landscape and topography
Vineyards sit on lake shores and hillsides around Zurich, Schaffhausen, Thurgau and Graubünden, among others.

## Soil and geology
Limestone, molasse, clay and glacial deposits, with more alpine soils in Graubünden.

## Climate
Cooler than the French-speaking cantons, with the Föhn wind bringing warm autumn days in the east.

## History
Winegrowing is old here, but plantings shrank with the rise of cheaper imports and later recovered thanks to better Pinot Noir.

## Production and wine styles
Pinot Noir, called Blauburgunder locally, makes the best reds, especially in the Bündner Herrschaft. Müller-Thurgau, called Riesling-Silvaner, and Pinot Gris make light whites.

## Key wines
- Bündner Herrschaft Pinot Noir
- Zurich Pinot Noir
- Schaffhausen Blauburgunder

## What to look for
- Fine, light Pinot Noir.
- Little leaves the country.
