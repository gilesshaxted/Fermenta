# Thracian Valley

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Bulgaria's largest and warmest wine region, home to the native red Mavrud and most of the country's big reds.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Bulgaria]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Warm continental
> - **Soils:** clay, loam, alluvial, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Mavrud]], [[Rubin]], [[Pamid]], [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Merlot]], [[Syrah]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad plain and low hills in southern Bulgaria, between the Balkan range to the north and the Rhodope mountains to the south.

## Soil and geology
Mostly deep, fertile clay and loam soils, with alluvial and sandy soils in places.

## Climate
Warm, with hot, dry summers and mild winters, which suits late-ripening reds.

## History
Thracians were making wine in this area in antiquity. Soviet-era state wineries dominated the 20th century, and since the 1990s private estates have been raising quality.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly reds, led by Mavrud, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah, with some Rubin and Pamid. Whites such as Chardonnay and Misket Cherven are also made.

## Key wines
- Thracian Valley Mavrud
- Thracian Valley Cabernet Sauvignon
- Thracian Valley Rubin
- Thracian Valley Merlot

## What to look for
- Dark, plummy reds with firm tannins.
- Mavrud is the local red to seek out.
