# Douro

> [!abstract] At a glance
> One of the world's oldest demarcated wine regions, with steep terraced vineyards, Port and powerful dry reds.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Portugal]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** schist, granite, terraced schist
> - **Main grapes:** [[Touriga Nacional]], [[Touriga Franca]], [[Tempranillo|Tinta Roriz]], [[Tinta Barroca]], [[Tinto Cão]]
> - **Styles:** [[Port (style)|Port style]]

## Landscape and topography
A dramatic river valley carved through mountains, with vines on steep terraces rising from the river, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

## Soil and geology
Schist, which shatters into layers that roots can penetrate, and some granite.

## Climate
Very hot and dry in summer, cold in winter, with big differences between the river valley and the higher slopes. The Serra do Marão shelters it from Atlantic rain.

## History
In 1756 the Marquis of Pombal demarcated the Douro, one of the first controlled wine regions in the world. The British merchants of Porto shaped Port wine trade, and a revival of dry Douro table wine began in the 1990s.

## Production and wine styles
Port, a fortified sweet wine, from Touriga Nacional, Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz and others. Dry Douro reds and whites are now a major focus.

## Key wines
- Douro red
- Douro white
- Vintage Port

## What to look for
- Red and black fruit, schist minerality and herbs.
- The three Douro sub-regions are Baixo Corgo, Cima Corgo and Douro Superior.
