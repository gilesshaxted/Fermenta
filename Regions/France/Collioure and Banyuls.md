# Collioure and Banyuls

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Dramatic terraced schist slopes dropping to the sea, producing dry Collioure and fortified Banyuls.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Roussillon]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** schist
> - **Main grapes:** [[Grenache]], [[Mourvèdre]], [[Syrah]], [[Carignan]]
> - **Styles:** [[Vin doux naturel]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep, narrow terraces called feixes, held by dry-stone walls, plunging into the Mediterranean at the border with Spain.

## Soil and geology
Dark schist, which retains heat and gives minerality.

## Climate
Mediterranean, with the Tramontane wind, sea breeze and very low rainfall.

## History
Banyuls is one of France's oldest fortified wine areas, and Collioure was once known for anchovies and Fauvist painters.

## Production and wine styles
Collioure is dry red, rosé and white. Banyuls is a fortified sweet wine from Grenache, often aged in oak or in sun-exposed demijohns.

## Key wines
- Collioure rouge
- Banyuls
- Banyuls Grand Cru
- Collioure blanc

## What to look for
- Collioure is dark, spicy and savoury.
- Banyuls offers chocolate, fig, walnut and coffee notes.
