# Sauternes

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The world's most famous botrytised sweet wine: honeyed, luscious and balanced by fine acidity. Barsac sits inside it.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Bordeaux]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** gravel, sand, clay, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sémillon]], [[Sauvignon Blanc]]
> - **Styles:** [[Sauternes (style)|Sauternes style]], [[Noble rot wines]]

## Landscape and topography
Low rolling hills on the left bank of the Garonne, where the small, cool Ciron river joins it.

## Soil and geology
Gravel, sand and clay over limestone, with drainage that suits white varieties.

## Climate
Autumn mornings bring mist where the cool Ciron meets the warmer Garonne, encouraging noble rot (Botrytis cinerea). Sunny afternoons then dry the grapes, concentrating sugars.

## History
The 1855 Classification ranked Château d'Yquem alone as Premier Cru Supérieur, above the First Growths of Sauternes and Barsac.

## Production and wine styles
Sémillon makes up most of the blend, with Sauvignon Blanc and a little Muscadelle. Grapes are picked in several passes through the vineyard, only when affected by botrytis, so yields are very low.

## Key wines
- Château d'Yquem
- Château Climens (Barsac)
- Château Suduiraut
- Château Rieussec

## What to look for
- Apricot, honey, saffron and marmalade, with acidity to match.
- Half bottles are common, and the best wines age for decades.
