# Jurançon

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Sweet and dry whites from the Pyrenean foothills, with brilliant acidity and tropical fruit.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[South-West France]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** clay, pebbles, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Petit Manseng]], [[Gros Manseng]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep, high-altitude slopes with the Pyrenees as a backdrop.

## Soil and geology
Clay and pebble-rich soils on a limestone base.

## Climate
Maritime but warmed by the foehn wind from the Pyrenees, which dries grapes on the vine.

## History
Henri IV was baptised with Jurançon wine, the story goes. Passerillage, drying the grapes on the vine, is the traditional method for sweet wine.

## Production and wine styles
Jurançon (sweet, from Petit Manseng) and Jurançon Sec (dry, from Gros Manseng).

## Key wines
- Jurançon (moelleux)
- Jurançon Sec

## What to look for
- Pineapple, mango, honey, ginger and zingy acidity.
- Sweet Jurançon is excellent with blue cheese and foie gras.
