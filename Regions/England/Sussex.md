# Sussex

> [!abstract] At a glance
> England's sparkling wine heartland, with South Downs chalk that echoes Champagne.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[England]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Cool maritime
> - **Soils:** chalk, greensand, clay, flint
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Pinot Meunier]], [[Bacchus]]
> - **Styles:** [[English sparkling wine]]

## Landscape and topography
Rolling chalk hills of the South Downs and the Weald, with south-facing slopes and a mild coastal influence.

## Soil and geology
Chalk and greensand, with clay and flint. The Downs belong to the same chalk belt that runs under Champagne.

## Climate
Cool maritime, with English Channel influence. A run of warm summers has helped quality.

## History
Nyetimber planted Chardonnay and Pinot Noir in 1988 and led a wave of ambitious sparkling wine producers. Ridgeview and Rathfinny followed. Sussex now has its own protected designation.

## Production and wine styles
Traditional-method sparkling wine from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier, plus some still wines such as Bacchus.

## Key wines
- Sussex sparkling
- Blanc de Blancs
- Blanc de Noirs
- Rosé

## What to look for
- Apple, citrus and brioche with high acidity.
- English sparkling wine often does well against Champagne in blind tastings.
