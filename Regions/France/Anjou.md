# Anjou

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A varied area of schist and limestone, making dry and sweet Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Franc and rosé.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Loire Valley]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** schist, tuffeau, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chenin Blanc]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Gamay]]
> - **Styles:** [[Noble rot wines]]

## Landscape and topography
Rolling hills and river valleys around Angers on the Loire and its tributaries, the Layon and the Aubance.

## Soil and geology
Dark schist in the west, known as the Anjou Noir, and pale tuffeau limestone in the east.

## Climate
Maritime, with the Armorican massif sheltering the vineyards from Atlantic rain.

## History
The sweet wines of the Coteaux du Layon were prized in northern Europe, and the region's rosés have a long history. Dry Chenin and Savennières are now increasingly fashionable.

## Production and wine styles
Chenin Blanc for dry whites (Savennières, Anjou Blanc) and sweet whites (Coteaux du Layon, Quarts de Chaume). Cabernet Franc for reds and rosé, and Grolleau for Rosé d'Anjou.

## Key wines
- Savennières
- Coteaux du Layon
- Quarts de Chaume
- Anjou Rouge
- Rosé d'Anjou

## What to look for
- Savennières is dry, steely and long-lived.
- Sweet Chenin is lighter and fresher than Sauternes, and ages brilliantly.
