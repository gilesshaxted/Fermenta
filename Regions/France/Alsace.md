# Alsace

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A sunny, sheltered strip east of the Vosges with some of France's most aromatic whites, labelled by grape.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** granite, limestone, marl, sandstone, volcanic, loess
> - **Main grapes:** [[Riesling]], [[Gewürztraminer]], [[Pinot Gris]], [[Muscat]], [[Pinot Blanc]], [[Sylvaner]], [[Pinot Noir]]
> - **Styles:** [[Crémant]], [[Noble rot wines]]

## Landscape and topography
A narrow strip of vineyards on the eastern foothills of the Vosges mountains, with the Rhine plain to the east.

## Soil and geology
One of the most geologically diverse regions in the world: granite, volcanic, sandstone, limestone, marl and loess, often in the same village.

## Climate
Continental and one of the driest regions in France, thanks to the rain shadow of the Vosges. Warm, sunny autumns allow slow ripening.

## History
The area has been French and German in turn, which explains the Germanic grape names, tall flute bottles and half-timbered villages. Wine was the area's pride long before borders shifted.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly white. The varieties are listed on the label, which makes it easy to understand. Styles are mostly dry, with Vendange Tardive (late harvest) and Sélection de Grains Nobles (nobly rotted) sweet wines. Crémant d'Alsace is the sparkling.

## Key wines
- Riesling
- Gewürztraminer
- Pinot Gris
- Muscat
- Crémant d'Alsace
- Vendange Tardive
- Sélection de Grains Nobles

## What to look for
- Check the sweetness: not all Alsace is dry, and labels do not always say.
- The 51 Grand Cru vineyards carry their name on the bottle.
