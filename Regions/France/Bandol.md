# Bandol

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Mourvèdre at its finest: dark, tannic, age-worthy reds from terraced hills above the sea, plus classy rosé.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Provence]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** limestone, clay, sandstone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Mourvèdre]], [[Grenache]], [[Cinsault]]
> - **Styles:** [[Rosé]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep terraces called restanques, in a natural amphitheatre facing the Mediterranean.

## Soil and geology
Limestone, clay and sandstone terraces with low fertility.

## Climate
Hot, dry Mediterranean, with sea breezes. Mourvèdre needs this heat to ripen fully.

## History
Mourvèdre came to Bandol with Phoenician traders. The appellation was established in 1941, and reds must age in oak for at least 18 months.

## Production and wine styles
Reds are at least 50% Mourvèdre, typically 70% or more. Rosés are also notably structured.

## Key wines
- Bandol rouge
- Bandol rosé
- Bandol blanc

## What to look for
- Black fruit, leather, herbs and wild thyme.
- A red that needs time, and loves game and slow-cooked lamb.
