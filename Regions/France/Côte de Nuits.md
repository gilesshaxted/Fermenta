# Côte de Nuits

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The heartland of red Burgundy, where nearly all the great Pinot Noir Grand Crus sit.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Burgundy]]
> - **Climate:** Semi-continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, marl, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Pinot Noir]], [[Chardonnay]]

## Landscape and topography
A narrow east-facing strip of slope between Dijon and Corgoloin, with the best vineyards on the middle of the slope.

## Soil and geology
Limestone and marl with varying clay and iron, the proportions changing metre by metre.

## Climate
Semi-continental, with the slope giving good exposure to the morning sun and some shelter from the west.

## History
Cistercian monks built the walled Clos de Vougeot, and villages added the name of their finest vineyard to their own, such as Gevrey-Chambertin and Vosne-Romanée.

## Production and wine styles
Almost entirely Pinot Noir, with tiny amounts of white from Chardonnay, such as Musigny Blanc.

## Key wines
- Gevrey-Chambertin
- Chambolle-Musigny
- Vosne-Romanée (including Romanée-Conti)
- Nuits-Saint-Georges
- Morey-Saint-Denis
- Vougeot

## What to look for
- Gevrey is muscular, Chambolle is perfumed and delicate, Vosne is silky and spicy.
- Grand Cru prices reach extreme levels, so Fixin and Marsannay offer a way in.
