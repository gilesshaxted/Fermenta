# Maipo Valley

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Chile's most famous region for Cabernet Sauvignon, just south of Santiago, with vines rising into the Andes foothills.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Chile]]
> - **Part of:** [[South America]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** alluvial, clay, gravel, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Carmenère]], [[Merlot]], [[Syrah]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad valley with vineyards from the city outskirts to the Andean foothills.

## Soil and geology
Alluvial soils with gravel and clay, plus limestone in places.

## Climate
Warm, dry and sunny, with cooling influence from the mountains at night.

## History
Vines were planted in the 19th century, and Concha y Toro and Santa Rita are historic names. Many pre-phylloxera vines survive because Chile avoided the pest.

## Production and wine styles
Cabernet Sauvignon leads, with Carmenère, Merlot and Syrah.

## Key wines
- Maipo Cabernet Sauvignon
- Alto Maipo Cabernet

## What to look for
- Blackcurrant, mint and structure.
- Alto Maipo in the foothills gives the finest wines.
