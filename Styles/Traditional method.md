# Traditional method

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Sparkling wine made by a second fermentation in the same bottle you drink from.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Category:** Method
> - **Colour:** Varies
> - **Sweetness:** Varies
> - **Country:** Various
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Pinot Meunier]], [[Chenin Blanc]]
> - **Regions:** [[Champagne]], [[Limoux]], [[Loire Valley]], [[England]]

## How it's made
Still base wine is bottled with sugar and yeast and sealed. The second fermentation makes bubbles, the dead yeast cells are left to age on lees, and the sediment is later frozen out and removed (disgorging).

## Where it's made
Used for Champagne, Cava, Franciacorta, Crémant, English sparkling wine and many others around the world.

## What it tastes like
- Brioche, biscuit, toast from lees
- Fine, persistent bubbles

## Pairs with
- Aperitif
- Seafood
- Fried food

## Good to know
- Champagne producers cannot call their method the champagne method outside the region, hence méthode traditionnelle.
- Long lees ageing is the key to flavour.
