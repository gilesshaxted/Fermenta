# Corsica

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A mountain island in the Mediterranean with distinctive native grapes and wines that taste of the maquis.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** granite, schist, limestone, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sciaccarello]], [[Sangiovese|Niellucciu]], [[Vermentino]], [[Grenache]], [[Muscat]]

## Landscape and topography
Mountains rising from the sea, with vineyards on the coast and in hill valleys, many facing the sea.

## Soil and geology
Granite in the west and south, schist in the east and north, and limestone around Patrimonio.

## Climate
Mediterranean, hot and dry, with sea breezes and cooler nights in the hills.

## History
Greek and Roman settlers planted vines, and later Genoese rule brought Italian grapes. Corsican varieties such as Niellucciu (related to Sangiovese) and Vermentino show the Italian connection.

## Production and wine styles
Reds and rosés from Niellucciu, Sciaccarello and Grenache, and whites from Vermentino. Muscat du Cap Corse is a sweet speciality.

## Key wines
- Patrimonio
- Ajaccio
- Vin de Corse
- Muscat du Cap Corse

## What to look for
- Herbs, wild scrub and a saline note.
- Vermentino and rosé shine with seafood.
