# Languedoc

> [!abstract] At a glance
> France's biggest wine region by area, a patchwork of Mediterranean landscapes with a huge range of styles.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** limestone, schist, clay, gravel, sandstone, basalt
> - **Main grapes:** [[Grenache]], [[Syrah]], [[Mourvèdre]], [[Carignan]], [[Cinsault]], [[Vermentino]], [[Chardonnay]]
> - **Styles:** [[Pétillant naturel]], [[Vin doux naturel]], [[Rosé]], [[Natural wine]], [[GSM]], [[Carbonic maceration]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad arc along the Mediterranean from the Rhône to the Pyrenees, with coastal plains, garrigue hills, mountain valleys and old river terraces.

## Soil and geology
Highly varied. Limestone, schist, clay, gravel, sandstone and basalt can be found within the same appellation.

## Climate
Mediterranean, with hot dry summers, mild winters, and wind (Tramontane and Mistral) that keeps vines healthy. Altitude cools some vineyards.

## History
Wine has been made here since the Greeks and Romans. In the 19th and 20th centuries the area produced vast quantities of cheap wine. Since the 1980s a quality revolution has brought attention to old-vine Carignan, new appellations and independent growers.

## Production and wine styles
Red blends of Grenache, Syrah, Mourvèdre, Carignan and Cinsault, plus whites from Grenache Blanc, Vermentino, Chardonnay and more. IGP Pays d'Oc offers freedom with varietal wines, while appellations such as Pic Saint-Loup and Faugères are more strictly regulated.

## What to look for
- Appellations vary hugely, so learn a few names rather than the region as a whole.
- Excellent value, and many exciting small producers.
