# Côtes de Provence

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The region's largest appellation, home of the pale, dry rosé style the world copies.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Provence]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** limestone, schist, granite, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Grenache]], [[Cinsault]], [[Syrah]], [[Mourvèdre]], [[Vermentino]]
> - **Styles:** [[Rosé]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad territory across the Var and surrounding areas, with sub-zones such as Sainte-Victoire, Fréjus and La Londe.

## Soil and geology
A mix of limestone in the north and crystalline schist and granite along the coast.

## Climate
Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and the Mistral.

## History
Provence rosé was long considered a holiday drink. Recent decades have seen a push for quality and styles that emphasise delicacy.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly rosé from Grenache, Cinsault, Syrah and Mourvèdre, with some red and white. Pressing is gentle to keep the colour pale.

## Key wines
- Côtes de Provence rosé
- Côtes de Provence blanc (Vermentino)

## What to look for
- Strawberry, citrus peel and herbal freshness.
- Drink young and well chilled.
