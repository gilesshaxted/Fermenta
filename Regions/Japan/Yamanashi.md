# Yamanashi

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Japan's historic wine heartland west of Tokyo, home of the Koshu grape.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Japan]]
> - **Part of:** [[Asia]]
> - **Climate:** Humid continental
> - **Soils:** volcanic, alluvial, gravel, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Koshu]], [[Muscat Bailey A]], [[Delaware]], [[Chardonnay]], [[Merlot]], [[Cabernet Sauvignon]]

## Landscape and topography
A mountain basin around Kofu, ringed by high peaks including Mount Fuji, with vineyards on alluvial fans and slopes.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic and alluvial soils, often free draining.

## Climate
Warm and humid in summer with a rainy season, so vines are often trained on overhead pergolas to keep fruit off the ground and allow airflow.

## History
Wine making in Japan is usually traced to this area in the late nineteenth century. Koshu has been grown here for a very long time.

## Production and wine styles
Light, dry whites from Koshu, plus reds from Muscat Bailey A and Bordeaux varieties. Sparkling and orange wines are also made.

## Key wines
- Koshu
- Yamanashi Muscat Bailey A
- Yamanashi red blends

## What to look for
- Most of Japan's wineries are in this prefecture.
- Koshu is subtle, so look for dry, fresh styles.
