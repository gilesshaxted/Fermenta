# Napa Valley

> [!abstract] At a glance
> California's most famous wine valley, defined by powerful Cabernet Sauvignon grown from valley floor to mountain slopes.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[USA]]
> - **Part of:** [[North America]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** alluvial, volcanic, clay, gravel, loam
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Chardonnay]], [[Merlot]], [[Sauvignon Blanc]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Zinfandel]]
> - **Styles:** [[Bordeaux blend]]

## Landscape and topography
A narrow valley about 30 miles long, between the Mayacamas Mountains to the west and the Vaca range to the east, with vineyards from the valley floor up to mountain benches.

## Soil and geology
An unusually varied patchwork: alluvial fans, gravel and loam on the valley floor, volcanic soils on the eastern hills and sedimentary soils to the west.

## Climate
Warm, dry Mediterranean summers, with cooling fog and wind from San Pablo Bay in the south. Carneros is much cooler than Calistoga at the northern end.

## History
Wine grew from the 1860s, was halted by phylloxera and Prohibition, and was revived after the 1960s. The 1976 Judgment of Paris put Napa on the world stage.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly red, led by Cabernet Sauvignon in Bordeaux-style blends, with Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc.

## Key wines
- Napa Cabernet Sauvignon
- Napa red blends
- Napa Chardonnay

## What to look for
- Ripe black fruit, cedar and structure.
- Look for sub-appellations such as Oakville or Stags Leap District on the label.
