# Sicily

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Italy's biggest island, with sun-baked vineyards, volcanic Etna and a long fortified wine tradition.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** volcanic, limestone, clay, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Nero d'Avola]], [[Nerello Mascalese]], [[Grillo]], [[Carricante]], [[Frappato]], [[Catarratto]]
> - **Styles:** [[Marsala (style)|Marsala style]], [[Passito]]

## Landscape and topography
Mountains, hills and coastal plains, with Mount Etna dominating the east.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic soils on Etna, limestone and clay inland, sandy soils on the coast.

## Climate
Hot Mediterranean, with strong sun and sea breezes. Altitude cools Etna.

## History
The Greeks introduced viticulture. Marsala, a fortified wine, became popular in the late 18th century, thanks to the English merchant John Woodhouse. In the 21st century, Sicily has become a quality leader.

## Production and wine styles
Nero d'Avola, Nerello Mascalese, Frappato, Grillo, Carricante, Catarratto, Zibibbo. Styles include still wines, sweet Passito di Pantelleria and Marsala.

## Key wines
- Etna Rosso
- Cerasuolo di Vittoria
- Marsala
- Passito di Pantelleria
- Nero d'Avola

## What to look for
- Etna is the exciting area, nicknamed the Burgundy of the Mediterranean.
- Marsala is more than a cooking wine.
