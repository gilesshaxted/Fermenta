# Aegean Islands

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Scattered islands including Lemnos, Samos, Paros and Rhodes, each with local grapes and styles.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Greece]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** volcanic, limestone, schist, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Limnio]], [[Muscat of Alexandria]], [[Muscat]], [[Assyrtiko]], [[Mandilaria]]

## Landscape and topography
Dry, windy islands scattered across the Aegean, with vines often in low bush form and terraces on slopes.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic soils, limestone and schist, depending on the island.

## Climate
Hot, dry and windy, so grapes are often grown close to the ground.

## History
Samos is famous for its sweet Muscat wines and its mountain terraces. Lemnos makes ancient Limnio wines, and Paros has Mandilaria.

## Production and wine styles
Muscat on Samos, Limnio on Lemnos, Mandilaria on Paros, and a mix elsewhere.

## Key wines
- Samos Muscat
- Lemnos Limnio
- Paros Mandilaria

## What to look for
- Sweet or herbal, with fresh sea air.
- Ancient grapes and island character.
