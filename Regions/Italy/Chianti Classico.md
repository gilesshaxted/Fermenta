# Chianti Classico

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The historic heart of Chianti, with a black rooster on the neck label and Sangiovese in the lead.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Tuscany]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** galestro, alberese, clay, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sangiovese]]

## Landscape and topography
Hills between Florence and Siena, rising to about 600 metres, with woods, olive groves and vineyards.

## Soil and geology
Galestro (schist-like clay) and alberese (limestone), which give structure and acidity.

## Climate
Mediterranean, with the higher altitude cooling summer nights.

## History
Chianti Classico was defined in 1716 by Cosimo III de' Medici, and the Gallo Nero (black rooster) consortium formed in 1924. The Gran Selezione top tier was added in 2014.

## Production and wine styles
At least 80% Sangiovese, now with no white grapes allowed. Tiers are Annata, Riserva and Gran Selezione, and there are now named UGAs (sub-zones) such as Greve and Radda.

## Key wines
- Chianti Classico
- Chianti Classico Riserva
- Chianti Classico Gran Selezione

## What to look for
- Cherry, tomato leaf, leather and tea.
- High acidity makes it a classic with tomato-based dishes and roast meat.
