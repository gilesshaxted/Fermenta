# Canary Islands

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Atlantic volcanic islands with ungrafted old vines, often trained along the ground, producing unusual wines.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Spain]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Subtropical
> - **Soils:** volcanic, ash, basalt
> - **Main grapes:** [[Listán Negro]], [[Palomino|Listán Blanco]], [[Malvasia|Malvasía]], [[Albillo]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep volcanic slopes on islands such as Tenerife, Lanzarote and La Palma, with vineyards at up to 1,700 metres.

## Soil and geology
Volcanic ash, lava and basalt, often with black picón stones to hold moisture.

## Climate
Subtropical but cooled by Atlantic trade winds. Lanzarote is extremely dry and windy.

## History
Canary wine was a big export, and Shakespeare mentions it. Phylloxera never reached the islands, so ancient vines survive.

## Production and wine styles
Many local grapes. In Lanzarote, vines grow in hollows (hoyos) with stone walls to protect from the wind.

## Key wines
- Tenerife Listán Negro
- Lanzarote Malvasía Volcánica
- La Palma Malvasía

## What to look for
- Smoky, mineral and distinct.
- Volcanic salinity is typical.
