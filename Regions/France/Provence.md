# Provence

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The world's rosé capital, with sun, sea and herb-scented landscapes, and some serious reds and whites too.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** limestone, clay, schist, sand, granite
> - **Main grapes:** [[Grenache]], [[Syrah]], [[Mourvèdre]], [[Cinsault]], [[Vermentino]]
> - **Styles:** [[Rosé]]

## Landscape and topography
A sweep of coast, plains, limestone ridges and hills running from the Rhône delta to the Italian border.

## Soil and geology
Limestone in the west and centre, crystalline schist and granite in the Massif des Maures and around the coast.

## Climate
Mediterranean, with long hot summers, mild winters, abundant sunshine and the Mistral wind.

## History
Greek settlers from Phocaea planted vines near Marseille around 600 BC, making Provence the oldest wine region in France. Rosé has been its signature for centuries.

## Production and wine styles
About 90% of production is rosé: pale, dry and made mainly from Grenache, Cinsault and Syrah. Reds and whites are more modest but include great examples at Bandol and Cassis.

## Key wines
- Côtes de Provence rosé
- Bandol
- Cassis
- Bellet
- Palette
- Coteaux d'Aix-en-Provence

## What to look for
- Pale pink usually means dry and fresh, not sweet.
- For serious Provence, look at Bandol.
