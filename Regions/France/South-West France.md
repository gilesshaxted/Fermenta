# South-West France

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A scattered but exciting group of regions with local grapes found nowhere else, from black Malbec to sweet Jurançon.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** clay, limestone, gravel, sand, black wine soils
> - **Main grapes:** [[Malbec]], [[Tannat]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Merlot]], [[Petit Manseng]], [[Gros Manseng]], [[Sauvignon Blanc]], [[Sémillon]]

## Landscape and topography
A huge area from the Dordogne to the Pyrenees, with river valleys, foothills and plateaux.

## Soil and geology
Very varied, with limestone, clay, gravel, sand and iron-rich soils.

## Climate
Mostly maritime with some continental influence, and warmer, drier conditions towards the Pyrenees.

## History
Medieval wine merchants from Bordeaux restricted the sale of wines from upriver, which helped local styles to develop in isolation. Local grapes survived because of that.

## Production and wine styles
Reds based on Malbec (Cahors), Tannat (Madiran), Négrette (Fronton) and Bordeaux grapes (Bergerac). Whites include Jurançon and Gaillac. Production is mostly small-scale.

## Key wines
- Cahors
- Madiran
- Jurançon
- Bergerac
- Gaillac
- Monbazillac
- Irouléguy

## What to look for
- Plenty of values here, with little hype.
- Local grapes such as Tannat, Négrette and Duras are worth trying.
