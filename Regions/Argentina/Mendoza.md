# Mendoza

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Argentina's wine heartland, producing most of the country's wine at the foot of the Andes.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Argentina]]
> - **Part of:** [[South America]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** alluvial, sand, gravel, limestone, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Malbec]], [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Bonarda]], [[Chardonnay]], [[Tempranillo]]

## Landscape and topography
A high desert plain at 600 to 1,500 metres, with the Andes to the west providing snowmelt for irrigation.

## Soil and geology
Mostly alluvial: sand, gravel and clay washed from the Andes, with limestone in some high sites.

## Climate
Very dry and sunny, with big day to night swings. Altitude moderates the heat, and hail and frost are real risks.

## History
Spanish settlers planted vines in the 16th century. Italian and French immigrants in the 19th century brought Malbec and modern methods, and quality leapt after the 1990s.

## Production and wine styles
Malbec dominates, with Cabernet Sauvignon, Bonarda, Chardonnay and Tempranillo.

## Key wines
- Mendoza Malbec
- Mendoza Cabernet Sauvignon
- Mendoza red blends

## What to look for
- Plum, violet and spice in Malbec.
- Altitude is key: higher vineyards give more freshness.
