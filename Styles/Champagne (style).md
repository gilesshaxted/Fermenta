# Champagne (style)

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The benchmark traditional method sparkling wine, from Chardonnay and the two Pinots.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Category:** Sparkling
> - **Colour:** White
> - **Sweetness:** Dry
> - **Country:** France
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Pinot Meunier]]
> - **Regions:** [[Champagne]], [[Montagne de Reims]], [[Côte des Blancs]], [[Vallée de la Marne]], [[Côte des Bar]]

## How it's made
Base wines are blended, bottled with sugar and yeast, then re-fermented in the bottle. The wine ages on its lees for at least 15 months for non vintage, and longer for vintage. The sediment is removed and a dosage tops the bottle up.

## Where it's made
Only wine from the Champagne region can use the name. The Montagne de Reims leans to Pinot Noir, the Côte des Blancs to Chardonnay, and the Vallée de la Marne to Pinot Meunier.

## What it tastes like
- Green apple, lemon, brioche
- Toasted almond and biscuit with age
- Fine, persistent bubbles

## Pairs with
- Oysters
- Fried food
- Soft cheese
- Salty snacks

## Good to know
- Blanc de Blancs is made only from white grapes, and is mostly Chardonnay. Blanc de Noirs is made only from black grapes.
- Brut is the most common dryness level.
- Most houses blend many vintages for a consistent house style.
