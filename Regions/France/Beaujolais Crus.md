# Beaujolais Crus

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The ten villages that make Beaujolais's most serious wines, each with its own style.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Beaujolais]]
> - **Climate:** Semi-continental
> - **Soils:** granite, schist
> - **Main grapes:** [[Gamay]]
> - **Styles:** [[Carbonic maceration]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep, granite-based hills in the northern half of the region, with village names on the label instead of Beaujolais.

## Soil and geology
Mostly granite and schist, with pockets of volcanic rock and clay that change the style from Cru to Cru.

## Climate
Semi-continental, with the hills sheltering the vineyards.

## History
Moulin-à-Vent is named after an old windmill, and Morgon's Côte du Py is a well-known hillside. The Crus have been trading on their reputations, and rising prices reflect a quality revival.

## Production and wine styles
100% Gamay, vinified by carbonic maceration or by more traditional Burgundian methods for the serious Crus.

## Key wines
- Fleurie (floral, delicate)
- Morgon (structured)
- Moulin-à-Vent (long-lived)
- Brouilly
- Saint-Amour
- Juliénas

## What to look for
- The ten Crus are Brouilly, Côte de Brouilly, Chénas, Chiroubles, Fleurie, Juliénas, Morgon, Moulin-à-Vent, Régnié and Saint-Amour.
- Moulin-à-Vent and Morgon can age and begin to resemble Pinot Noir.
