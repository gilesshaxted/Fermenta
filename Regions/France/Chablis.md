# Chablis

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The northernmost corner of Burgundy: steely, flinty Chardonnay shaped by cold and fossil-rich limestone.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Burgundy]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, marl
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]]

## Landscape and topography
Gently rolling hills around the Serein river valley, with the Grand Crus on a single south-west facing slope above the town.

## Soil and geology
Kimmeridgian limestone and marl full of tiny fossilised oyster shells, which many credit for the wine's mineral, saline edge. Petit Chablis sits on younger Portlandian limestone.

## Climate
Cool and continental, with a real risk of spring frost. Growers use heaters, sprinklers and smudge pots to protect the buds.

## History
Chablis once supplied Paris, with wine shipped down the Yonne river. Frost, phylloxera and war shrank the vineyard area in the 20th century, and it has since been replanted and expanded.

## Production and wine styles
100% Chardonnay. The levels are Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru and Chablis Grand Cru. The seven Grand Cru climats are Bougros, Les Preuses, Vaudésir, Grenouilles, Valmur, Les Clos and Blanchots. Many wines are made in stainless steel, others in old oak.

## Key wines
- Chablis
- Chablis Premier Cru (such as Montmains, Fourchaume)
- Chablis Grand Cru
- Petit Chablis

## What to look for
- Green apple, lemon, wet stone and sea-shell salinity.
- Excellent with oysters, shellfish and goat's cheese.
