# Alsace Grand Cru

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The 51 named Grand Cru vineyards, restricted to four noble grapes, with distinctive soils and bold wines.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Alsace]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** granite, limestone, marl, sandstone, volcanic
> - **Main grapes:** [[Riesling]], [[Gewürztraminer]], [[Pinot Gris]], [[Muscat]]

## Landscape and topography
Individual slopes scattered along the foothills, including Schlossberg, Rangen, Brand, Hengst, Schoenenbourg and Sommerberg.

## Soil and geology
A different geology at each site, from the granite of Schlossberg to the volcanic soils of Rangen and the limestone of Hengst.

## Climate
Continental and dry, with sunny slopes.

## History
The Grand Cru system dates from 1975 and expanded to 51 sites. It is often debated, as some argue it favours vineyard over producer.

## Production and wine styles
Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat, with some blends and Pinot Noir permitted at certain sites.

## Key wines
- Schlossberg
- Rangen
- Brand
- Hengst
- Schoenenbourg
- Sommerberg

## What to look for
- Riesling Grand Crus are racy and mineral, built to age.
- Gewürztraminer Grand Crus can be rich and lushly spiced.
