# Qvevri wine

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Georgian wine fermented and aged in large buried clay vessels, a tradition recognised by UNESCO.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Category:** Method
> - **Colour:** Varies
> - **Sweetness:** Dry
> - **Country:** Georgia
> - **Main grapes:** [[Rkatsiteli]], [[Mtsvane]], [[Kisi]], [[Khikhvi]], [[Saperavi]]
> - **Regions:** [[Kakheti]], [[Kartli]], [[Imereti]]

## How it's made
Grapes, juice, skins and sometimes stems go into a clay qvevri that is buried in the ground, where the stable temperature lets the wine ferment and rest without intervention. White grapes treated this way become amber or orange wines with firm tannin and a nutty, savoury character. The qvevri is sealed and left through winter before the wine is drawn off.

## Where it's made
Across Georgia, especially in Kakheti in the east, and in Imereti where less skin contact is traditional.

## What it tastes like
- Dried apricot, tea, walnut
- Firm tannin on white grapes
- Savoury, earthy depth

## Pairs with
- Roast lamb
- Walnut sauces
- Hard cheese
- Georgian khachapuri

## Good to know
- Imereti wines usually see less skin contact than Kakheti wines.
- Producers elsewhere now use amphorae in the same spirit.
