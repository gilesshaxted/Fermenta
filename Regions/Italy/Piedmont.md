# Piedmont

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The foot of the Alps, home of Barolo and Barbaresco, with thick autumn fog, long ageing and serious food culture.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, marl, clay, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Nebbiolo]], [[Barbera]], [[Dolcetto]], [[Muscat|Moscato]], [[Cortese]], [[Arneis]]
> - **Styles:** [[Asti and Moscato d'Asti]]

## Landscape and topography
Hills of the Langhe, Roero and Monferrato below the Alps, with vineyards on south-facing slopes, usually at 150 to 450 metres, and the Alps visible beyond.

## Soil and geology
Marine sediments from an ancient sea: calcareous marl, sandstone, clay and sand, which vary across the hills and shape each cru.

## Climate
Continental, with cold winters, warm summers and autumn fog (nebbia) that gives Nebbiolo its name and a long ripening season.

## History
Piedmont's Savoy rulers led the unification of Italy. Barolo was once a sweet wine, until Marchesa Falletti and the winemaker Louis Oudart helped make it dry in the 19th century. A generation of producers in the 1980s and 1990s argued about modern versus traditional styles.

## Production and wine styles
Nebbiolo for Barolo, Barbaresco and Roero, Barbera and Dolcetto for everyday reds, Moscato for the sweet Asti, Cortese for Gavi, and Arneis for Roero. Small family estates are the norm.

## Key wines
- Barolo
- Barbaresco
- Barbera d'Asti
- Dolcetto d'Alba
- Gavi
- Moscato d'Asti
- Roero Arneis

## What to look for
- Nebbiolo is pale in colour but high in tannin and acidity, and needs years.
- Barbera and Dolcetto drink earlier and cost less.
