# Bordeaux

> [!abstract] At a glance
> France's largest fine-wine region, built on blends of Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, plus great sweet and dry whites.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** gravel, clay, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Merlot]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Petit Verdot]], [[Sauvignon Blanc]], [[Sémillon]]
> - **Styles:** [[Crémant]], [[Sauternes (style)|Sauternes style]], [[Bordeaux blend]]

## Landscape and topography
Low-lying and gently rolling, set around the Gironde estuary and the Garonne and Dordogne rivers. Most vineyards sit under 100 metres, and the rivers divide the region into the Left Bank, the Right Bank and the Entre-Deux-Mers between them.

## Soil and geology
Gravel banks left by the rivers on the Left Bank, clay and limestone on the Right Bank, and a mix of sand, clay and limestone elsewhere. Gravel drains well and holds heat, which suits Cabernet Sauvignon. Clay and limestone suit Merlot and Cabernet Franc.

## Climate
Maritime. The Atlantic and the pine forest of the Landes to the west moderate temperatures. Rain and rot at harvest are the main risks, and vintage variation is large, which is why blending is so central to the region.

## History
Wine shipped from Bordeaux to England under English rule from 1152 to 1453, when 'claret' became a British habit. Dutch engineers drained the marshes of the Médoc in the 17th century, and the 1855 Classification ranked the top Médoc and Sauternes châteaux for the Paris Exhibition. Phylloxera and mildew struck hard in the late 19th century.

## Production and wine styles
Reds are blends of Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc, with smaller amounts of Petit Verdot and Malbec. Whites are Sauvignon Blanc, Sémillon and Muscadelle. Wine is usually sold through the négociants of La Place de Bordeaux, with en primeur sales of young wine each spring.

## Key wines
- Left Bank reds (Cabernet-led)
- Right Bank reds (Merlot-led)
- Sauternes and Barsac (sweet)
- Dry white Bordeaux
- Crémant de Bordeaux

## What to look for
- Left Bank labels lean towards Cabernet, Right Bank labels towards Merlot.
- The generic Bordeaux and Bordeaux Supérieur appellations are the everyday, value end.
- Classifications differ by area: 1855 for the Médoc and Sauternes, separate lists for Graves and Saint-Émilion, and none for Pomerol.
