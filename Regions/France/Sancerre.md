# Sancerre

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Mineral, vivid Sauvignon Blanc from hilltop vineyards, plus some Pinot Noir reds and rosés.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Loire Valley]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, flint, marl
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sauvignon Blanc]], [[Pinot Noir]]

## Landscape and topography
A hilltop town and rolling hills on the left bank of the upper Loire in central France.

## Soil and geology
Three soil types: caillottes (white limestone pebbles), terres blanches (Kimmeridgian clay-marl) and silex (flint), giving wines of different weight and minerality.

## Climate
Continental, with colder winters and warmer summers than the western Loire. Frost is a risk in spring.

## History
Sancerre was once better known for red wine. After phylloxera, growers replanted with Sauvignon Blanc and the white wines became the star.

## Production and wine styles
Sauvignon Blanc for whites, Pinot Noir for rosé and red. Usually stainless steel, sometimes oaked, often with fine lees contact.

## Key wines
- Sancerre blanc
- Sancerre rosé
- Sancerre rouge

## What to look for
- Gooseberry, citrus, flint and chalk, with a refreshing line.
- A classic match with goat's cheese, especially Crottin de Chavignol from the nearby village of Chavignol.
