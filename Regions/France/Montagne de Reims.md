# Montagne de Reims

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A forested plateau south of Reims, famous for powerful Pinot Noir that gives Champagne its structure.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Champagne]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** chalk, clay, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Pinot Noir]], [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Meunier]]
> - **Styles:** [[Champagne (style)|Champagne style]]

## Landscape and topography
A wooded dome rising to about 280 metres, with vineyards on its north, east and south slopes around Verzenay, Verzy, Bouzy and Ambonnay.

## Soil and geology
Chalk with clay and sand, giving body and drainage.

## Climate
Cool and continental, with the forest moderating conditions and protecting from wind.

## History
Grand Cru villages here include Verzenay, Verzy, Mailly, Ambonnay and Bouzy. Bouzy is also known for a still red wine, Bouzy Rouge.

## Production and wine styles
Pinot Noir dominates, with Chardonnay on some slopes. The wines give body and richness to blends.

## Key wines
- Blanc de Noirs
- Bouzy Rouge (still)
- Ambonnay cuvées

## What to look for
- Red apple, red fruit and vinous depth.
- Often the structure behind long-ageing Champagnes.
