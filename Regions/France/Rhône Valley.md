# Rhône Valley

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A long river valley with two personalities: steep, Syrah-led slopes in the north and warm, Grenache-led blends in the south.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Varies
> - **Soils:** granite, schist, limestone, clay, galets roulés, sand
> - **Main grapes:** [[Syrah]], [[Grenache]], [[Mourvèdre]], [[Viognier]], [[Marsanne]], [[Roussanne]]
> - **Styles:** [[Vin doux naturel]], [[GSM]]

## Landscape and topography
The Rhône runs south from Lyon to Avignon. The north is narrow, with terraced slopes plunging to the river, while the south opens into wide plains, plateaux and hills beneath Mont Ventoux.

## Soil and geology
Granite and schist in the north, with limestone at Condrieu's edges. The south is a mosaic of limestone, clay, sand and the famous rounded stones (galets roulés) left by ancient glaciers and the river.

## Climate
Cooler, continental in the north. Mediterranean in the south, with hot, dry summers and the Mistral wind drying the vines and reducing disease.

## History
The Romans planted vines on the northern slopes. The popes moved to Avignon in the 14th century and built a summer residence at Châteauneuf, giving the appellation its name. In 1923 Baron Le Roy helped to write the rules that shaped the French appellation system, starting at Châteauneuf-du-Pape.

## Production and wine styles
Syrah rules the north, with Viognier in Condrieu. The south blends Grenache, Syrah and Mourvèdre, with up to 13 grape varieties allowed in Châteauneuf-du-Pape. Côtes du Rhône is the large regional appellation, Villages and named Crus step up in quality.
