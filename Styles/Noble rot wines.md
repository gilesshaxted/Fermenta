# Noble rot wines

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Sweet wines from grapes affected by the beneficial mould Botrytis cinerea.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Category:** Sweet
> - **Colour:** White
> - **Sweetness:** Sweet
> - **Country:** Various
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sémillon]], [[Furmint]], [[Riesling]], [[Chenin Blanc]], [[Gewürztraminer]], [[Pinot Gris]], [[Welschriesling]]
> - **Regions:** [[Sauternes]], [[Tokaj]], [[Anjou]], [[Alsace]], [[Mosel]], [[Neusiedlersee]], [[Burgenland]]

## How it's made
In humid mornings followed by dry afternoons, the mould pierces the skins and dehydrates the grapes. The result is concentrated sugar, acid and flavour: honey, saffron and marmalade.

## Where it's made
Sauternes, Loire (Coteaux du Layon), Alsace, Germany, Austria and Tokaj all make noble rot wines.

## What it tastes like
- Honey, marmalade, saffron
- Dried apricot
- Fresh acidity balancing sweetness

## Pairs with
- Foie gras
- Blue cheese
- Fruit tarts

## Good to know
- Germans call it Trockenbeerenauslese at its richest.
- Alsace uses the term Sélection de Grains Nobles.
