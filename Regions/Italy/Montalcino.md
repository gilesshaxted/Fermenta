# Montalcino

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Home of Brunello, a rich, long-lived Sangiovese Grosso from a warm hill south of Siena.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Tuscany]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** galestro, alberese, clay, sand, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Sangiovese]]

## Landscape and topography
A hilltop zone around the town of Montalcino, with vineyards at 150 to 500 metres and Monte Amiata to the south.

## Soil and geology
Varied: galestro and clay, sand and limestone, across different slopes.

## Climate
Warmer and drier than Chianti, with Monte Amiata blocking rain.

## History
Brunello was developed by Ferruccio Biondi-Santi in the late 19th century. The zone received DOCG status in 1980, the first in Italy. A scandal in 2008 over blending (Brunellopoli) led to stronger controls.

## Production and wine styles
100% Sangiovese (called Brunello locally). Brunello di Montalcino needs five years' ageing (six for Riserva), and Rosso di Montalcino is the younger, cheaper sibling.

## Key wines
- Brunello di Montalcino
- Brunello Riserva
- Rosso di Montalcino

## What to look for
- Dark cherry, leather, spice and a long finish.
- Rosso di Montalcino is a good early-drinking way in.
