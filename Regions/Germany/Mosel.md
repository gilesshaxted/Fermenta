# Mosel

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Steep slate river valleys producing delicate, low-alcohol Riesling with piercing acidity.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Germany]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Cool continental
> - **Soils:** slate, devonian slate, quartzite
> - **Main grapes:** [[Riesling]], [[Müller-Thurgau]], [[Pinot Blanc]], [[Pinot Noir]]
> - **Styles:** [[Icewine and Eiswein]], [[Noble rot wines]]

## Landscape and topography
Dramatic bends of the Mosel river, with some of Europe's steepest vineyards (up to about 65 degrees) facing south to catch the sun.

## Soil and geology
Blue, grey and red Devonian slate, which holds heat and gives a smoky, mineral character.

## Climate
Cool, with the river reflecting light and slate soaking up heat. Autumn mists favour late harvests.

## History
Romans planted vines on the slopes, and the Mosel became a prestigious region in the 19th century. Steep sites and handwork make the wines costly to produce.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly Riesling, ranging from bone-dry to lusciously sweet. The Prädikat system (Kabinett, Spätlese, Auslese, and so on) is based on grape ripeness, not sweetness, though ripeness often means more sugar. Trocken means dry.

## Key wines
- Mosel Kabinett
- Spätlese
- Auslese
- Trockenbeerenauslese
- Eiswein
- Grosses Gewächs

## What to look for
- Peach, lime, wet slate and floral notes.
- Low alcohol (often 8 to 10 percent) and capable of long ageing.
