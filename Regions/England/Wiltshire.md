# Wiltshire

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Chalk downland around Salisbury Plain, the Marlborough Downs and the Vale of Pewsey, with the same underlying chalk that helps the south-east vineyards.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[England]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Cool maritime
> - **Soils:** chalk, flint, greensand, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Pinot Meunier]], [[Bacchus]]
> - **Styles:** [[English sparkling wine]]

## Landscape and topography
Wide chalk downs, ancient hill forts and gentle valleys, with Avebury and Stonehenge set within the landscape.

## Soil and geology
Chalk with flint, and greensand and clay in the vales.

## Climate
Cool, with a drier, more continental feel than the coast, so site selection (south-facing, sheltered, frost-free) matters.

## History
Wiltshire's chalk landscape has been farmed for thousands of years. A few small vineyards and wine ventures have appeared across the county in recent decades, and local wine interest is growing.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly small-scale sparkling and still wines from cool-climate grapes.

## Key wines
- Wiltshire sparkling
- Wiltshire still whites

## What to look for
- Chalky freshness and crisp acidity.
- A county to watch for small producers.
