# Rioja

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Spain's most famous red wine region, known for oak-aged Tempranillo and a clear ageing classification.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Spain]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** clay, limestone, ferrous clay, alluvial gravel
> - **Main grapes:** [[Tempranillo]], [[Grenache|Garnacha]], [[Graciano]], [[Carignan|Mazuelo]], [[Macabeo|Viura]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad valley along the Ebro river, sheltered by the Sierra de Cantabria to the north, with vineyards at about 300 to 700 metres.

## Soil and geology
Clay and limestone, ferrous red clay and alluvial gravel along the river, varying through the three zones.

## Climate
A blend of Atlantic, continental and Mediterranean influences. The Sierra protects from the north and the Atlantic cools the west.

## History
Rioja was the first Spanish DOCa, in 1991. Bordeaux winemakers arrived in the 19th century after phylloxera hit France, bringing barrel ageing that shaped Rioja's style.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly Tempranillo, blended with Garnacha, Graciano and Mazuelo. Ageing sets the label: Joven (young), Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva, with American or French oak. Modern producers now emphasise single villages and vineyards.

## Key wines
- Rioja Crianza
- Rioja Reserva
- Rioja Gran Reserva
- Rioja Blanco
- Vino de Pueblo
- Viñedo Singular

## What to look for
- The ageing term tells you the style: Crianza is fruitier, Gran Reserva is silky and savoury.
- Traditional wines show vanilla and coconut from American oak.
