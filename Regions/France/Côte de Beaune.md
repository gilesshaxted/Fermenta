# Côte de Beaune

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Home of the world's greatest white Burgundy, plus elegant reds in Volnay, Pommard and Beaune.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Burgundy]]
> - **Climate:** Semi-continental
> - **Soils:** limestone, marl, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Noir]]

## Landscape and topography
Wider, gentler slopes than the Côte de Nuits, with vineyards mostly facing east and south-east around Beaune and Meursault.

## Soil and geology
Limestone and marl. White marl and pale limestone on the Meursault and Montrachet slopes suit Chardonnay.

## Climate
Semi-continental. Hail is a recurring threat, and vintages vary markedly.

## History
The Hospices de Beaune, a charity hospital founded in 1443, still holds an annual wine auction on the third Sunday of November. Montrachet and Corton-Charlemagne are among the oldest named vineyards in France.

## Production and wine styles
Chardonnay for the famous whites and Pinot Noir for the reds. Corton is the only red Grand Cru in the Côte de Beaune, alongside the white Corton-Charlemagne.

## Key wines
- Meursault
- Puligny-Montrachet
- Chassagne-Montrachet
- Corton-Charlemagne
- Pommard
- Volnay
- Beaune

## What to look for
- Meursault is rounder and nuttier, Puligny is tighter and more mineral.
- Volnay is lighter and silkier, Pommard firmer and more structured.
