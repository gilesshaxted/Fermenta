# Hermitage

> [!abstract] At a glance
> A single granite hill above Tain-l'Hermitage that makes some of France's longest-lived Syrah and rich white wines.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Rhône Valley]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** granite, loess, gravel
> - **Main grapes:** [[Syrah]], [[Marsanne]], [[Roussanne]]

## Landscape and topography
One steep south-facing hill, with parcels named Les Bessards, Le Méal, L'Hermite and others.

## Soil and geology
Granite in the west (Les Bessards), mixed with loess, clay and gravel on other lieux-dits.

## Climate
Continental, sheltered and warm on the slope.

## History
Thomas Jefferson praised Hermitage. In the 19th century it was among the most expensive wines in Europe and was used to boost Bordeaux.

## Production and wine styles
Syrah for red, and Marsanne with some Roussanne for white. Reds need years to soften, whites can age remarkably.

## Key wines
- Hermitage rouge
- Hermitage blanc
- Vin de paille (rare straw wine)

## What to look for
- Dark fruit, smoke, leather and graphite for red.
- Beeswax, almond and honey for white.
