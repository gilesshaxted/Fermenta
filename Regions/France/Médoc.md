# Médoc

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The northern half of the long Left Bank peninsula between the Gironde and the Atlantic, making sound, Merlot-friendly reds at fair prices.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Bordeaux]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** gravel, clay, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Merlot]], [[Cabernet Franc]], [[Petit Verdot]]
> - **Styles:** [[Bordeaux blend]]

## Landscape and topography
Flat to gently undulating, with low gravel mounds near the estuary and flatter, heavier ground further north. Pine forest and the Atlantic lie to the west.

## Soil and geology
Clay and limestone mixed with gravel, heavier than in the Haut-Médoc to the south. Merlot does particularly well on the clay.

## Climate
Maritime, with the estuary and the ocean softening extremes. Slightly cooler and later ripening than the southern Médoc.

## History
The marshland was drained by Dutch engineers in the 17th century, opening the area to vines. The Cru Bourgeois system later recognised good estates outside the 1855 Classification.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly red, with Merlot often playing a bigger part than in the communes to the south, blended with Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot.

## Key wines
- Médoc AOC
- Cru Bourgeois estates

## What to look for
- Often good value compared with the famous communes to the south.
- Look for Cru Bourgeois on the label as a quality guide.
