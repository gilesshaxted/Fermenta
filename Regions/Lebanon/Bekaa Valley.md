# Bekaa Valley

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Lebanon's main wine region, a high inland valley that grows most of the country's grapes.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Lebanon]]
> - **Part of:** [[Asia]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** limestone, gravel, clay, alluvial
> - **Main grapes:** [[Cabernet Sauvignon]], [[Cinsault]], [[Syrah]], [[Obaideh]], [[Merwah]], [[Carignan]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad valley between the Mount Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges, with vineyards at roughly 900 to 1,200 metres.

## Soil and geology
Limestone, gravel and clay, with free-draining stony patches.

## Climate
Warm, dry summers with cool nights from the altitude, and cold winters with some snow. Rain is low and vines are often irrigated.

## History
Vines have been grown here since antiquity, with Roman temples at Baalbek close by. Modern estates such as Ksara, Kefraya and Chateau Musar built the region's reputation in the twentieth century, often through the difficult years of civil war.

## Production and wine styles
Mostly reds from Cabernet Sauvignon, Cinsault, Syrah and Carignan, with Bordeaux and Rhone style blends. Whites include Obaideh, Merwah, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc.

## Key wines

## What to look for
- Ripe fruit with fresh acidity from altitude.
- Chateau Musar is the famous name to try.
