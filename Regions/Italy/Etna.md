# Etna

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Mineral, high-altitude wines from the slopes of Europe's most active volcano.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Sicily]]
> - **Climate:** Mediterranean
> - **Soils:** volcanic, lava, ash
> - **Main grapes:** [[Nerello Mascalese]], [[Carricante]]

## Landscape and topography
Terraced slopes of Mount Etna, at up to 1,000 metres, with vineyards on every side.

## Soil and geology
Black volcanic soils of lava, ash and pumice, with different eruptions creating distinct 'contrade' (crus).

## Climate
Mediterranean but cool at altitude, with big day-night swings and late harvests.

## History
Etna has grown wine since Greek times. Many old ungrafted vines remain. International attention grew from the 2000s, as winemakers recognised the potential.

## Production and wine styles
Etna Rosso is Nerello Mascalese-led (with Nerello Cappuccio). Etna Bianco is Carricante-led.

## Key wines
- Etna Rosso
- Etna Bianco
- Etna Rosato

## What to look for
- Pale, perfumed reds with red cherry, smoke and ash.
- Compared to Pinot Noir and Nebbiolo.
