# Emilia-Romagna

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The land of Parmesan, balsamic and prosciutto, with fizzy Lambrusco and Romagna Sangiovese.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[Italy]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Temperate
> - **Soils:** alluvium, clay, sand, limestone
> - **Main grapes:** [[Lambrusco]], [[Sangiovese]], [[Trebbiano]], [[Albana]]
> - **Styles:** [[Lambrusco (style)|Lambrusco style]]

## Landscape and topography
A broad plain around the Po, with the Apennine foothills to the south and the Adriatic to the east.

## Soil and geology
Alluvial soils on the plains, clay and limestone on the hills.

## Climate
Temperate to continental, with hot summers and foggy winters.

## History
Lambrusco, once seen as an export commodity, is being revived by quality producers. Romagna has long been Sangiovese territory.

## Production and wine styles
Lambrusco (sparkling red, mostly dry) around Modena and Reggio, and Sangiovese di Romagna in the east.

## Key wines
- Lambrusco di Sorbara
- Lambrusco Grasparossa
- Sangiovese di Romagna
- Albana di Romagna

## What to look for
- Dry Lambrusco is a superb partner for cured meats and Parmesan.
- The sweet, cheap versions are a different story.
