# Muscadet

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Crisp, saline white from the Atlantic coast, born to partner oysters and seafood.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Loire Valley]]
> - **Climate:** Maritime
> - **Soils:** granite, gneiss, schist
> - **Main grapes:** [[Melon de Bourgogne]]

## Landscape and topography
Gently undulating land around Nantes and the Sèvre and Maine rivers, near the Atlantic coast.

## Soil and geology
Granite, gneiss and schist, giving the wines a salty, mineral tension.

## Climate
Maritime, with a cool Atlantic climate and mild, damp conditions.

## History
A severe frost in 1709 destroyed most of the vines, and the hardy Melon de Bourgogne replaced them. The Dutch trade in Muscadet followed.

## Production and wine styles
Melon de Bourgogne only. Muscadet Sèvre-et-Maine is the largest area. Ageing sur lie, leaving the wine on its fine lees until bottling, adds texture and a slight creaminess.

## Key wines
- Muscadet Sèvre-et-Maine sur lie
- Muscadet Côtes de Grandlieu
- Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire
- Crus communaux (Clisson, Gorges, Le Pallet)

## What to look for
- Lemon, saline and green apple, with a slight sparkle of freshness.
- Basic Muscadet is simple, but the sur lie and cru communaux wines are far more serious.
