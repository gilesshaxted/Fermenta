# Savoie

> [!abstract] At a glance
> Alpine vineyards producing crisp, light whites and the peppery red Mondeuse, ideal for fondue.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Alpine
> - **Soils:** limestone, scree, clay, schist
> - **Main grapes:** [[Jacquère]], [[Altesse]], [[Mondeuse]], [[Gamay]], [[Chasselas]]

## Landscape and topography
Steep, small plots on mountain slopes at 250 to 500 metres, scattered between the Alps and the Lac du Bourget.

## Soil and geology
Glacial deposits, limestone scree, clay and schist.

## Climate
Alpine, with cold winters and short, warm summers. The lakes moderate temperatures.

## History
Savoie was part of the Kingdom of Sardinia until 1860, which explains its Italian-influenced grape names. The wines were long consumed locally by skiers and farmers.

## Production and wine styles
Light, fresh whites from Jacquère and Altesse, sparkling wines, and reds from Mondeuse and Gamay.

## Key wines
- Apremont
- Chignin-Bergeron (Roussanne)
- Roussette de Savoie
- Mondeuse Arbin
- Crémant de Savoie

## What to look for
- Crisp and mineral with apple and citrus.
- A natural match for raclette, tartiflette and fondue.
