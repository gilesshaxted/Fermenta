# Champagne

> [!abstract] At a glance
> The world's benchmark for sparkling wine, made by the traditional method in a cold, chalky northern region.

> [!info] Quick facts
> - **Country:** [[France]]
> - **Part of:** [[Europe]]
> - **Climate:** Continental
> - **Soils:** chalk, limestone, marl, clay
> - **Main grapes:** [[Chardonnay]], [[Pinot Noir]], [[Pinot Meunier]]
> - **Styles:** [[Champagne (style)|Champagne style]], [[Traditional method]]

## Landscape and topography
Gently rolling hills and river valleys around Reims and Épernay, with vineyards on slopes of roughly 90 to 300 metres, and a southern outpost in the Aube.

## Soil and geology
Chalk dominates in the Marne, with marl, clay and sand in places. The chalk stores water, reflects light and is easy to dig into cellars, and the Aube has older Kimmeridgian limestone and clay.

## Climate
Cool and continental, one of the coldest wine regions in France. Grapes ripen with high acidity, ideal for sparkling wine, and frost and rain are constant risks.

## History
Benedictine monks, including the cellarer at Hautvillers, helped to develop sparkling wine, although the Dom Pérignon legend is exaggerated. Madame Clicquot is credited with early work on riddling in the early 19th century. The region was legally defined in 1927 and devastated in both World Wars.

## Production and wine styles
Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier, by the traditional method with a second fermentation in the bottle. Non-vintage wines age at least 15 months and vintage wines at least three years. Styles run from Brut Nature to Doux, with blanc de blancs, blanc de noirs and rosé options.

## What to look for
- Look for NM (négociant-manipulant, a house) or RM (récoltant-manipulant, a grower) on the label.
- Brut Nature is bone dry, Brut is the standard, Demi-Sec is noticeably sweet.
